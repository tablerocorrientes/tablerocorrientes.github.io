Climate outlook for November to February
Issued 22 October 2026
Long-range forecast overview
The long-range forecast for November to January shows:
Rainfall is likely to be above average for large parts of Australia, below average in parts of the far north and south-east.
Daytime temperatures are very likely to be above average across most of Australia.
Overnight temperatures are very likely to be above average across most of Australia.
Rainfall—Summary
Above average rainfall likely for large parts of Australia, below average in parts of the far north and south-east
November to January
November to January rainfall is likely (60% to greater than 80% chance) to be above average across most of southern and central Western Australia extending into southern parts of the Northern Territory, most of South Australia, southern Queensland and north-eastern New South Wales.
The wet signal is more pronounced in the west earlier in the season but strengthens in the east during December.
There is an increased chance of unusually high rainfall 1 (40 to 60% chance) across southern parts of Western Australia.
Below average rainfall is likely (60 to 80% chance) for Tasmania, and parts of southern Victoria and the tropical far north.
There is an increased chance of unusually low rainfall 1 (40 to 60% chance) for parts of central and south-eastern Tasmania and north-western coastlines of Western Australia. .
During this outlook period, northern Australia typically experiences increasing humidity, storms, and showers.
The ACCESS-S rainfall forecast is consistent with international guidance, with most models forecasting wetter than average conditions in the south-west and below average rainfall across the far north. There is some variation in the likelihood of above average rainfall across the east between models.
1 Unusually low/high rainfall is that in the driest/wettest 20% of November to January records between 1981 and 2018. Select your location on the map for more localised information.
Temperature—Summary
Above average temperatures likely across Australia
November to January
November to January maximum temperatures are very likely to be above average (greater than 80% chance) across much of Australia.
There is an increased likelihood (over 50% chance) of unusually high maximum temperatures 2 across much of Australia, with the highest chances (over 70%) across eastern parts of New South Wales and Victoria, eastern Tasmania, south-west Western Australia and Queensland's far north.
Minimum temperatures are very likely (greater than 80% chance) to be above average across much of Australia.
The chance of unusually high minimum temperatures 2 is very high (over 80% chance) for much of the country. This is more than 4 times the normal likelihood.
The ACCESS-S forecast for temperatures is consistent with most international models, however ACCESS-S predicts higher chances of above average temperatures compared to some other models.
2 Unusually high maximum and minimum temperatures are those in the warmest 20% of November to January days and nights, respectively, between 1981 and 2018. Select your location on the map for more localised information.
We use our long-range model, ACCESS-S, to forecast up to 4 months ahead. The model simulates how the atmosphere and oceans may evolve, using millions of observations from satellites, land stations, and ocean instruments.
We also track global climate indicators to help understand long-term weather patterns. These indicators mainly reflect interactions between the ocean and atmosphere.
Current climate indicators:
A strong El Niño is underway. All climate models, including the bureau's, forecast further warming of the tropical Pacific through the southern hemisphere spring, with El Niño likely to peak around November to December as a very strong event.
A strong El Niño signal in the tropical Pacific does not necessarily mean strong impacts on Australia's rainfall and temperature; ENSO is only one of several factors that can influence Australia’s seasonal weather and climate.
The Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) is currently neutral although weekly index values have been above the positive IOD threshold for 6 consecutive weeks. Models suggest a positive IOD is likely to develop during the southern hemisphere spring and persist into early summer, although there remains uncertainty in the strength and duration of the event.
The sea surface temperature (SST) analysis for the week ending 18 October 2026 shows waters around most of Australia's west, south and south-east are up to 3°C warmer than average. SSTs to Australia's north-east have cooled to 2 °C below average in some areas.
SSTs in the Australian region are forecast to warm over the coming months, particularly to Australia's west and in the Coral Sea. Warmer waters in the Australian region can enhance atmospheric moisture and energy that can fuel storms and rain systems, increasing potential for high intensity rainfall.
Global SSTs remain at record levels. September 2026 was the warmest September on record at 1.02 °C above the 1961–1990 average. It was also the second warmest month on record overall since 1900.
